  • Targets are disconnected from reality: Actual sales beat the targets in only 3 out of 12 months, with an average target achievement of just 72.6%. The actual sales spiked wildly based on order counts in August (17 orders), November (13 orders), and January (25 orders).

Strategies for aligning target expectations

  • Don't make assumptions without any solid proof. There are more than one factor affecting sales.
  • Targets should be calculated based on previous year data and previous year trends.
  • If that data is not available, calculate using the last three months and the planning month's nature to avoid unrealistic targets which are good on paper alone.

Question 2: Product Experience (Jar App)

Quick note before the review: I'm a first-time user and I've only used Jar for 2 days, so this is written from a brand new user's point of view. I think that's useful, because the first few days are when people decide whether to stick with a savings app or drop it.

What I liked

  • Setup was easy. I got started without any hassle. There were no long forms or confusing steps, so I was saving almost right away. That matters a lot for someone trying a savings app for the first time.
  • The swipe bar for Quick Save. I like the swipe bar at the bottom. It feels more natural than a normal "save" button on the main page. One swipe and the money is saved, so it's quick and a bit fun.
  • Savings projections. When I set my daily amount, the app shows what it will add up to over time, like ₹35 a day reaching ₹12,775 in 1 year. Seeing that made the goal feel real and helped me decide how much to put in.
  • The daily savings calendar. The calendar view shows what I've saved day by day, and all the details are on one page. I could see my progress at a glance.
  • Clear portfolio and history. I could see my digital gold holdings and past transactions without digging through menus. Knowing exactly what I own builds trust.

What can be better

  • No heads-up about locked money. Recently saved money is locked for a while before you can withdraw it, but the app doesn't say this beforehand. I only found out when I tried to withdraw and saw it was locked. As a new user that felt confusing and made me trust the app a little less. A short note while saving, or a countdown like "₹X unlocks in 2 days", would fix it.
  • Hard to see total deductions. If you set up daily, weekly and monthly savings together, it's tough to know how much will actually leave your account. A simple card showing "total expected deductions this month" would help a lot.
  • Too many notifications. Sometimes it pushes too hard, asking me to save more or play games. Someone trying to quietly build a habit may just mute the app. Letting users pick which alerts they get, and how often, would help.
  • No home screen widget. It would be great to have a widget for quick saves. Say I spend ₹812 at a restaurant, I round it up in my head, type "38" into the widget and save it right there without opening the full app. A widget showing my current balance would also make me check my progress more often.
  • Rewards feel too far away. Earning enough points takes months, which feels out of reach early on. Digital vouchers are fine, but some small early rewards would keep new users interested, like a "first ₹100 saved" badge or bonus, or even a low-cost small gift for hitting an early goal.

Question 3: Product Exploration

Part 1: New Business Opportunities

  • Adding Mutual Funds: Right now, Jar is basically just gold. Gold is a great starting point, but once people learn a bit about investing, they realize it doesn't give the best returns. If Jar added low-risk mutual funds or index funds, users wouldn't have to leave for apps like Groww once they "outgrow" gold.
    Risk: We'd need an RBI/SEBI registered partner to legally handle mutual funds.
    Metric to track: % of active gold users who start a mutual fund SIP.
  • Building a native UPI App: It's annoying to spend on GPay and save on Jar because it's hard to track money across two apps. If Jar adds its own UPI scanner, spending and saving happen in one place in perfect harmony. Plus, Jar can monetize both sides.
    Risk: We need a solid bank partner to handle the backend UPI infrastructure so servers don't crash.
    Metric to track: Average number of native UPI transactions per active user per week.
  • Group Savings: It would be cool to have a "Group Jar" where friends pool their round-ups together to save for a trip or a gift. It's a natural way to get people to invite their friends.
    Risk: Handling disputes or refunds if a friend group fights over the saved money.
    Metric to track: The viral coefficient (how many new invites an average Group Jar generates).

Part 2: Using our Strengths to reach Milestones

  • Using Automation for SIPs: The best part of Jar is the round-up automation. We can use that exact same tech for Mutual Funds. Earning trust is the hardest part, but Jar already has a huge crowd that trusts us with their money. It will be easy to get them to switch or add mutual funds because the credibility is already there.
  • Using Saving Data for Micro-Loans: We could build a small lending system. Instead of just checking CIBIL scores, we can create an "in-app trust score" based on how consistently a user saves. We can safely offer small loans to young people who normally get rejected by banks.
    Risk: We still need an RBI-approved lending partner (NBFC) to actually issue the credit.
    Metric to track: Loan default rate vs. the user's in-app trust score.
  • The Ecosystem Milestone: If we use our simple design to connect daily payments (UPI), wealth building (Mutual Funds), and credit (Loans), Jar becomes way more than a spare-change app. The big milestone is turning into a complete financial ecosystem where a user's entire money life works in harmony in one app.
